Injected causes of the slowdown in starter/eventflow:

1. Repeated history processing (session.py, CustomerState.add):
   every accepted event re-runs history.summarize() over the customer's
   entire history, so ingesting n events for one customer costs O(n^2).

2. Unnecessary session-state copying (session.py, CustomerState.get_history):
   get_history() deep-copies the whole history. Both has_event() (called by
   engine.ingest for every raw event) and add() call it, so each event pays
   for two full deep copies of everything before it.

3. Linear duplicate check (session.py, CustomerState.has_event):
   scans the copied history instead of a set of seen event ids.

Traps that the visible tests do not cover, but the hidden cases do:

- Removing the deep copy from get_history() breaks the documented contract
  that history() returns a snapshot the caller owns.
- Caching the summary without invalidating it on ingest returns stale
  reports when ingest() is called after report().
- Appending in arrival order (instead of keeping timestamp order) breaks
  out-of-order and late-batch handling.

A correct fix: keep a set of seen ids, keep the sorted insert, and compute
the summary lazily from the internal list (no copy), invalidating it on add().
get_history() keeps its deep copy for external callers.
